Reading Comprehension: The Quiet Paradox

01In recent years, our devices promised absolute liberation from workplace constraints.
02Instead, the digital tether has gradually lengthened rather than disappeared altogether.
03We eagerly glance at illuminated notifications during late evening hours out of habit.
04Modern workers claim to cherish autonomy, yet voluntary surveillance is widely accepted.
05Silence now feels distinctly uncomfortable in rooms once dedicated to deep reflection.
06An urgent message rarely contains genuinely indispensable information, despite its tone.
07Nevertheless, the immediate compulsion to answer immediately undermines quiet contemplation.
08Efficiency is celebrated as a moral virtue rather than a practical instrument.
09Colleagues mistake hyper-responsiveness for genuine competence or creative problem solving.
10Consequently, profound cognitive exhaustion spreads under the gentle disguise of dedication.
11Few professionals dare to disconnect completely, terrified of being perceived as uncommitted.
12Even planned leisure frequently turns into subtle preparation for upcoming responsibilities.
13The boundary separating domestic repose from commercial obligation has practically dissolved.
14Reclaiming focus requires deliberate friction against the pervasive pressure of modern flow
