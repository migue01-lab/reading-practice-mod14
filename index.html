# Reading Comprehension: Urban Wildlife Adaptation (Level B1+) Read the following passage carefully and evaluate the statements below as **TRUE**, **FALSE**, or **NOT GIVEN**. --- ### Reading Passage 1 In recent decades, metropolitan landscapes have witnessed an unprecedented influx of wild fauna. 2 Contrary to common belief, these animals are not merely straying from depleted natural reserves. 3 Instead, species such as urban foxes, raccoons, and corvids actively colonize residential areas. 4 This behavior is largely motivated by the abundance of easily accessible anthropogenic food waste. 5 However, human proximity introduces several unpredictable hazards, including vehicular traffic. 6 To cope with these anthropogenic threats, urban animals often adjust their circadian rhythms. 7 A notable example is the coyote, which increasingly adopts nocturnal habits in built-up districts. 8 Behavioral ecologists have also documented a marked rise in problem-solving persistence. 9 City-dwelling specimens tend to tackle unfamiliar obstacles far faster than rural counterparts. 10 While some biologists argue that cities act as evolutionary engines favoring distinct traits, 11 others warn that reliance on human leftovers could disrupt critical dietary requirements. 12 Despite these nutritional drawbacks, overall metropolitan animal densities continue to surge. 13 Consequently, municipal councils are being forced to rethink conventional sanitation designs. 14 Advanced animal-proof waste bins are now standard installations across multiple capitals. 15 Whether this ongoing coexistence remains sustainable in the long term remains an open question. --- ### Exercise: True, False, or Not Given 1. Wild creatures migrate to suburban neighborhoods solely because their natural environments have been destroyed. * **Answer:** FALSE * **Explanation:** Line 2 and 3 state: *"Contrary to common belief, these animals are not merely straying from depleted natural reserves. Instead, species [...] actively colonize residential areas."* The word *solely* makes the statement incorrect. 2. Readily available discarded groceries and leftovers encourage wildlife to settle in urban territories. * **Answer:** TRUE * **Explanation:** Line 4 explains that colonization is *"largely motivated by the abundance of easily accessible anthropogenic food waste."* 3. Noise pollution causes more stress to urban animals than speeding automobiles. * **Answer:** NOT GIVEN * **Explanation:** Line 5 mentions vehicular traffic as a hazard (*"unpredictable hazards, including vehicular traffic"*), but acoustic/noise pollution and comparative stress levels are never mentioned in the text. 4. Coyotes alter their sleep-wake cycles so they can hunt domestic pets late at night. * **Answer:** NOT GIVEN * **Explanation:** Line 7 notes that coyotes adopt nocturnal habits to cope with threats (*"adjust their circadian rhythms"*), but the passage never states that they do this to target domestic pets. 5. Research indicates that urban fauna demonstrate greater cognitive tenacity when facing novel challenges. * **Answer:** TRUE * **Explanation:** Lines 8–9 state: *"ecologists have also documented a marked rise in problem-solving persistence. City-dwelling specimens tend to tackle unfamiliar obstacles far faster than rural counterparts."* 6. Biologists have reached a unanimous consensus regarding the positive long-term impact of cities on wildlife evolution. * **Answer:** FALSE * **Explanation:** Lines 10–11 show a division of opinions (*"While some biologists argue... others warn"*), meaning there is no unanimous consensus. 7. Consuming human food scrap provides city wildlife with superior nutritional health compared to a natural diet. * **Answer:** FALSE * **Explanation:** Line 11 highlights warnings that this reliance *"could disrupt critical dietary requirements,"* and line 12 explicitly refers to these as *"nutritional drawbacks."* 8. Wildlife populations in urban centers have dropped sharply due to road accidents and poor nutrition. * **Answer:** FALSE * **Explanation:** Line 12 confirms the opposite: *"Despite these nutritional drawbacks, overall metropolitan animal densities continue to surge"* (surge means increase rapidly). 9. Modern animal-resistant garbage containers have already eliminated urban fox foraging in every European capital. * **Answer:** NOT GIVEN * **Explanation:** Line 14 mentions that *"Advanced animal-proof waste bins are now standard installations across multiple capitals,"* but it does not claim they have completely eliminated foraging, nor does it specify all European capitals. 10. Experts are completely confident that the mutual coexistence between urban dwellers and wildlife will endure indefinitely. * **Answer:** FALSE * **Explanation:** Line 15 concludes that *"Whether this ongoing coexistence remains sustainable in the long term remains an open question,"* which directly contradicts being "completely confident."
